# Lore — Vardelar (docs/design/Lore.md)

`BL-350`. **Status: SKELETON + 1st pass**, settled with the owner 2026-10-13. Only the name and the shape below are
decided; the sketched sections further down are drafts, and every other name (the lesser gods, the boss pieces, the
later races) is still open. Yours to edit.

## The name

**Vardelar** is the Creator, the god of all creation, and **the world is his body**. The game, the world and the dead god
share the one name. The working subtitle is *War of the Lesser Gods*.

Chosen over Orrath (one letter from *Norrath*, EverQuest's world), Aldurne (*Alduin*, Skyrim's World-Eater), Vaelora
(*Veldora*, Tensura), Valamers (*Valar*/*Valmar*, Tolkien's gods and their city) and Thricemade (it counts the races, and
more are coming). Faint echoes it carries: *Varda* (Tolkien) and *Vvardenfell* (Morrowind). Partial only, neither in the
same slot.

## The shape of the myth

1. **Vardelar** made the lesser gods, and then the world.
2. **The dispute.** The lesser gods are immortal and cannot truly kill one another, so they invent a *game* of war. Each
   makes a people to fight it:
   - the stone-god makes **men from stone and women from ash** (the `BL-354` seed: the myth says why there are two sexes);
   - the frost-god makes **the elves in the ice** (`BL-349`: the elves born in the ice);
   - the god below makes **the demons in the underworld** (`BL-349`: the demon summoned from the underworld).
3. **The murder.** Vardelar objects, so the lesser gods turn on him together and kill him, so that none stands above
   them. His body falls and becomes the world. "The bones of Vardelar" is the ground you walk on.
4. **World bosses are the pieces of Vardelar that refuse to die**: his Heart, his Eye, his Wrath, his Dream, and so on.
   Every lesser god sends its race against them before he can come together again. That is why every race hunts the
   same bosses, and why their loot is divine.
5. **Later races are already set up:**
   - a 4th: the wife of a lesser god, left out, makes her own people out of spite;
   - a 5th: the child of two lesser gods, who becomes their downfall.

## IP note

"One father god, his children each make a race" is close to IG's own creation story. Ideas are not protected, but it is
the "same slot" pattern `CLAUDE.md` warns about. **The two things that make it ours, and must stay:** the Creator was
*murdered* and the world *is his body*, and the races are *pieces in the gods' game*. Every god name passes the usual
test (word + same race + same role).

# Lore - Sketched:  ***Vardelar — Lore Foundation***(*War of the Lesser Gods*)

## Later peoples and new regions

The myth leaves room for new peoples without making them feel like additions to a finished list.

A later people might come from:

- the wife of a lesser god, left out, makes her own people out of spite
- a child of two lesser gods, who becomes their downfall
- a god who was excluded from the first war;
- the union of two gods who believed they could control what they made;
- a region of Vardelar’s body that changed after a Divine Piece awakened;
- a people who arose without any god’s design;
- an existing people transformed by generations in a new environment.
- a separation of elves - Options: forest elves, water elves, dark elves
- a separation of demons - Options: hellborn, lightborn, bloodhungry, warborn
- a separation of humans - Options: humans, highhumans, lesserhuman, dwarves, orks
- Lore of separations:
  - inner race dispute 
  - some want peace other war
  - some want to live among other races
  - some awakened and made own society
  - some left and made own society -> later evolutions/lifestyle changed their looks/race-like features

A future people should have their own account of their origin, homeland, and relationship to the gods. Their account may contradict the older myths. Contradiction can be part of the lore: the gods are unreliable narrators, and the peoples inherited different memories.

New regions can reveal different parts of the dead Creator. A place shaped by his bones may value ancestry and permanence; a coast formed from his blood may center migration, trade, and change; a region touched by his dreams may make memory and identity uncertain. These are starting points, not rules every resident has to follow.

## Expansion rule

Every new race, zone, god, or Divine Piece can answer one question and open another:

1. **What part of the world or myth does it reveal?**
2. **Who tells its story, and what do they want people to believe?**
3. **How does it change the balance between the gods and the peoples?**
4. **What remains uncertain for a future story?**

That gives Vardelar a complete central myth—the Creator was murdered, his body became the world, and the gods still use its peoples in their war—while leaving plenty of room for the game to grow.

## 1st pass:

### **The world that died**

Before there was earth, there was Vardelar: the first Creator. He made the lesser gods, then shaped a world for them to share. He gave them power to make, change, and rule—but none could take life from another god.

The lesser gods grew jealous of one another. Their quarrels could never end in victory, so they made a game of war. Each would shape a people, give them a homeland and a purpose, then send them against the others. The gods could settle their rivalries without risking their own lives.

Vardelar forbade the game. His creations were not pieces to be spent.

The gods agreed on one thing: while Vardelar lived, none of them could rule without limit. Together, they killed him.

Vardelar fell across the heavens. His body became the world: his bones became the mountains and deep stone; his blood ran into seas and rivers; his breath became the winds; his hair became the forests. His death made a home for every people—and left the gods free to continue their game.

They had mistaken death for an ending.

### **The first peoples**

The gods made their peoples from the world and from parts of themselves. These are the oldest accounts, told differently by each culture.

- **Humans**, made by the Stone God, were shaped from stone and awakened with ash. The old tale says stone gave them endurance while ash gave them the power to change. Humans disagree about what this means: some claim it is a promise of renewal; others say they were made to be used and replaced.
- **Elves**, made by the Frost God, were found in the oldest ice. They woke carrying memories that were not quite their own: fragments of the world before the murder. Some see this as a sacred inheritance; others fear the ice preserved grief along with memory.
- **Demons**, made by the God Below, were summoned from the underworld. Their origin is often called a curse by their enemies, but demons have their own accounts: the underworld was not a prison until the gods made it one.

Each people was made for the gods’ war. None is bound to remain in the role its maker intended.

### **The death that did not finish**

Vardelar’s body is vast, but it is not still. Beneath the surface, something remembers being alive.

His heart beats far below the world. His eye watches from a place no map agrees on. His wrath stirs beneath battlefields. His dreams seep into sleep and prophecy. These remnants are known as the **Divine Pieces**.

When a Piece awakens, the land around it changes. Old ruins surface. The dead may stir. A region’s weather, creatures, or magic can turn strange. The lesser gods send their peoples to find the Piece before their rivals do.

The gods call this a contest. Their armies call it war.

People who face a Divine Piece discover that it is neither a simple monster nor a treasure chest. It is a fragment of Vardelar’s will, distorted by death and centuries of divine conflict. Defeating one can quiet a threatened region, but it can also wake another. The divine loot taken from these encounters carries a remnant of Vardelar’s power—and sometimes a memory.

### **The lesser gods**

The names and details of the gods can be settled later. Their roles should give the myth different points of view, rather than making any one people wholly good or evil.

- **The Stone God** values endurance, duty, and survival. He says he gave humanity the strength to outlast the game.
- **The Frost God** values memory, patience, and preservation. She says the elves were made to remember what the others would rather forget.
- **The God Below** values transformation, freedom, and the power to endure hardship. He says the demons were made from what the other gods cast away.

These are the gods’ claims, not necessarily the truth. Each people contains believers, skeptics, loyalists, and those who reject their maker altogether.

The gods cannot truly kill one another. They can wound, bind, deceive, and diminish one another—but the game has no final victory. That is why they keep needing mortal armies.

### **Why the war continues**

Each god claims to protect their people from the others. Each also needs the war to continue: the Divine Pieces are dangerous, but they are the only known remnants of Vardelar’s original power.

Some gods want to gather the Pieces and use them to rule. Some want to keep Vardelar from ever returning. Some claim they want to restore him, though no one knows whether a restored Creator would forgive the murder—or the centuries of war that followed.

The peoples fight for more than their gods’ approval. They fight to defend homes, settle old grievances, claim resources, protect sacred places, and decide who gets to shape the future. A person may serve a god, defy one, or fight for reasons the gods do not understand.

### **The world’s unanswered question**

The deepest question in Vardelar is not whether its Creator can return. It is whether the world should let him.

If Vardelar awakens, the lesser gods may lose their power. The Divine Pieces may become whole again. The world may heal—or be remade by a Creator who remembers being betrayed.

No scripture agrees on what Vardelar wanted before his death. The Pieces may hold the answer, but each reveals only a fragment.
